# Retention comparisons

Read benchmarks.json for sourced reference points. Select by metric definition and population before comparing values. Publication year and observation year differ. These are context, not universal pass/fail thresholds. Match cohort entry, return event, unit, horizon, elapsed/calendar time, timezone, platform, geography, billing interval, company stage and inclusion filters. If material details are unknown, use directional context or unavailable; do not invent a precise percentile.

Daily consumer habits: exact-day and weekly core-value retention, cohort heatmap and active-days distribution. Workweek SaaS: weekly people/account retention, activation and seat adoption. Developer tools: retained projects/accounts delivering useful jobs; automated workloads and human dashboard visits differ. Episodic products: repeat use over a realistic opportunity cycle. Subscription products: behavioral return, paid renewal and revenue retention are separate metrics. Games: match genre/platform when data exists; all-games figures cannot establish a genre target.

Prefer comparisons with the product's own earlier matched cohorts when external fit is weak. Show denominator counts and uncertainty around the app estimate. Peer P25/P75 spread is variability across products, not a confidence interval for this app. Draw benchmark points only at published horizons; do not fabricate intermediate curves. Mark immature cohort cells unavailable. A tiny cohort is not a precise ranking.

Mechanics borrowed from games should support mastery, useful feedback, autonomy and valuable collaboration. More clicks or time do not establish more value. An efficient developer tool may reduce time spent. Treat habit loops and generic retention targets as hypotheses.
